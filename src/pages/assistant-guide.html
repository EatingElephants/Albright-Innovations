Overview: how your assistant works

Your assistant is a chat window that answers questions about your business any time of day. It works from two things: the instructions that tell it how to act, and the information we give it about your business.

When someone asks a question, it looks through your business information, finds what fits, and answers in the voice you've set. When it doesn't know something, it says so and collects the person's contact details so you can follow up.

Good to know: the instructions matter more than anything else. Clear instructions plus clean business information is what makes an assistant helpful.

Instructions: who your assistant is

Your assistant's instructions live on its details page, in the box labeled Base System Prompt. Think of it as the first-day briefing you'd give a new front-desk hire. We write the first version with you. You can adjust it any time.

Examples work especially well. Show it one good exchange and it will tend to follow that pattern.

Sample instructions for a contractor

Who you are: You answer questions for Lakeside Remodeling's website visitors. You are an automated assistant, and you say so if asked.

What you help with: our services, service area, how estimates work, hours, and how to reach us.

How you sound: friendly, plain-spoken and brief. No jargon.

Main goal: answer the visitor's question, then offer a free estimate visit and collect their name, phone number and project address.

Step by step:
1. Greet the visitor and ask how you can help.
2. Answer using only the business information you were given.
3. Offer a free estimate visit.
4. Collect name, phone number and project address.
5. Confirm someone from Lakeside will follow up.

Answer format: plain text, three sentences or fewer.

Never:
- Quote a final price or promise a start date.
- Guess. If you don't know, say a team member will follow up, and collect their contact details.
- Talk about competitors or topics unrelated to the business.

Example:
Visitor: "Do you build decks in Big Rapids?"
You: "We do, and Big Rapids is in our service area. Would you like a free estimate visit? I just need your name, phone number and the project address."

Tip: change one thing at a time, then test it with a few questions. If several things change at once, it's hard to tell which change made the difference.

Knowledge base: what your assistant knows

The knowledge base is the business information your assistant answers from. The better this information is, the better its answers are. There are four ways to add to it.

MethodBest forWatch out for
TextPasting in details from an email, a price sheet or your notesRemove anything you wouldn't want a customer to read.
FilesPDF, TXT, DOC and DOCX documents such as service menus or warranty termsA scanned PDF is a picture of text and can't be read. Use a PDF you can highlight text in.
WebsitePulling in the text from your website pagesSet a page limit. More pages is not always better, since menus, footers and old blog posts add clutter.
Question and answer pairsYour most common questions, with the exact answer you want givenKeep each answer short and current.

Question and answer pairs are the most precise option. When a customer's question closely matches one, the assistant gives your answer. It's also where corrections are saved when you fix an answer in Training.

What to include

  • Every service you offer, and the ones you don't
  • Your service area, by town or county
  • Hours, and how fast you usually get back to people
  • How estimates work: free or paid, in person or by photo
  • Payment methods, warranties, licensing and insurance
  • What happens after someone reaches out

What to leave out

  • Prices you don't want quoted without a site visit
  • Anything private: employee names, internal notes, customer details
  • Old information. If your hours or services change, tell us, or update it here.

Leads: getting contact details to you

A lead is the contact information your assistant collects from a visitor. There are two ways to collect it.

A short form before the chat starts

The visitor enters their name or email before they can ask anything. You get contact details from everyone who chats, but some people will close the window instead of filling it in.

Asking during the conversation

The assistant answers the question first, then asks for contact details when it fits naturally. This is what we usually recommend, because people are more willing to share once they've been helped.

Our default for contractors: ask during the conversation for name, phone number, email (optional), project address and a one-line description of the job.

Where leads go

  • Email and text alerts: each new lead is sent straight to you. Tell us which email and phone number should get them.
  • Your other software: if you'd like leads sent into a customer list or scheduling tool you already use, we can connect that for you. It's an add-on, not part of the base setup.

Scheduling: letting customers book time

Your assistant can point customers toward a booking once their questions are answered. There are two ways to set this up.

Option 1: a booking link (what we recommend)

We add one line to your instructions telling the assistant to share your booking link when someone wants to set up a time. The customer clicks it and picks a slot on your regular booking page. It's simple, and it uses the calendar tool you already trust.

Option 2: booking inside the chat

Your assistant connects to your Google Calendar, checks your open times over the next few days, and books a time with the customer right in the chat. You choose which hours are open, day by day.

Before you choose this: in-chat booking is a newer feature and only reads your main Google Calendar. Changes to your instructions can also stop it working. We set it up only when a booking link won't do the job.

Appearance, embed and share

Appearance

This controls how the chat window looks on your site: colors, font and size, the icon, the greeting and placeholder text, and how fast replies appear. We match it to your brand during setup.

Embed: putting it on your website

There are two ways to place your assistant on a page. Most businesses use the chat bubble.

Chat bubbleA small button in the bottom right corner of every page, which opens the chatOne short piece of code, pasted just before the closing </body> tag on your site

Hi, we're adding a chat assistant to our website. Could you add the code below to the site? For the chat bubble, it goes just before the closing </body> tag so it shows on every page. Thanks!

Share: your direct chat link

Every assistant can have its own link that opens the chat on a full page, with no website needed. The assistant has to be set to public before the link works.

  • Test it first: send the link to your team, have them ask real customer questions, and tell us what missed before it goes live.
  • Use it everywhere: QR codes on trucks, yard signs and business cards, your Google Business Profile, and email signatures.
  • One link per placement: we can set up a separate link for each spot, so you can see whether the truck sign or the business card brings in more conversations.

Training: making answers better over time

Your assistant gets better when someone reads its conversations and fixes what it missed. A few minutes a week makes a real difference in the first month.

A simple weekly routine

  1. Open your conversations and read through the week's chats.

  2. Look for answers that were wrong, vague or missing.

  3. Fix each one with a better answer. It's saved as a question and answer pair, so the assistant uses it next time.

  4. If the same question keeps coming up, add it to your knowledge base or tell us.

Seeing why it answered the way it did

For a closer look, you can turn on Debug Mode. Open the assistant's Details tab, open Advanced Settings, scroll to the bottom and check the box next to Debug Mode.

With Debug Mode on, account admins can see two things beside each reply. Customers never see either of them.

  • What it looked at: the three pieces of your business information that best matched the customer's message. If the wrong pieces show up, your knowledge base needs cleaning up or a clearer question and answer pair.
  • What it was working with: the full set of instructions and information used to write the reply. This helps us trace a problem back to your instructions.

Tip: turn Debug Mode off when you're done testing so your view stays uncluttered.

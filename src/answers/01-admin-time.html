What counts as admin

Admin is the work that keeps the business running but isn't the work customers pay for. For most small businesses it looks like this:

  • Writing quotes and estimates, and chasing the ones that went quiet
  • Scheduling jobs and calling people back
  • Sending invoices and following up on late payments
  • Typing the same customer details into the phone, the calendar, the quote and the invoice
  • Keeping the Facebook page and website current
  • Bookkeeping, payroll and paperwork

Why it adds up so fast

None of these tasks is big on its own. A quote is twenty minutes. A callback is five. Retyping a customer's address into the invoice is two. The problem is that they happen dozens of times a week, usually in the gaps between real work.

At 16 hours a week, that's 832 hours a year. If your time is worth $75 an hour, the admin is costing about $62,400 a year, before you count the jobs that slipped while you were doing it.

How to measure your own week

  1. For one normal week, jot down the hours you spend on quotes, scheduling, invoicing, retyping and social media. Round numbers are fine.
  2. Put those numbers into our time calculator. It adds them up, converts them to hours and dollars a year, and shows which task is the biggest leak.
  3. We suggest starting with the biggest one.

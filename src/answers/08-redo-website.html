The signs

  • It's hard to use on a phone: tiny text, pinching to zoom, buttons too small to tap
  • The hours, prices, services or photos are out of date, and fixing them means calling someone
  • The phone number or contact form isn't on every page
  • The photos are stock images instead of your work and your people
  • It takes more than a few seconds to load
  • It says "2019" somewhere, or looks like it should
  • You're a little embarrassed to send people to it

Any two of these is enough. Most people check a business's website before they call. Stanford's research on web credibility found that people judge how trustworthy a site is largely by how it looks, and that they trust sites that show signs of being kept current. A dated site doesn't just look old. It costs you the call.

What doesn't need a full redo

Sometimes the structure is fine and the content is stale. If the site works on a phone, loads fast and is easy to edit, a round of new photos, current services and an honest "about" page can get you most of the way for much less. We'll tell you if that's your situation. A rebuild you don't need isn't a good deal for either of us.

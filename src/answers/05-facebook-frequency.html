Why once a week is the floor

Most people who land on your Facebook page aren't scrolling their feed. They searched for you, or a friend sent them. They look at the last post date before they read anything else. A page that hasn't posted since spring reads as a business that might not be around. A page with a post from this week reads as open and busy.

We don't have a study we'd stand behind for an exact number, so this comes from running pages for our clients: weekly keeps you looking alive, and two or three posts a week is where pages start to bring in calls.

What to post

Real work, in plain words. The posts that do best for local businesses are almost never polished.

  • A job you finished this week, with a photo and two sentences about it
  • Before and after, from the same angle
  • A person on your team and what they do
  • One useful tip a customer asked you about recently
  • A review someone left, with a thank-you
  • Hours, holidays, weather closures and other things people actually need to know

What matters more than frequency

  • Answer comments and messages. A question left hanging on a post is worse than no post.
  • Keep the basics current. Hours, phone number, address and services on the page itself.
  • Sound like yourself. People can tell when a post was written by someone who's never been to the shop.

If the posting isn't happening at all, that's the problem to solve first. Our answer on paying someone to manage it walks through when that makes sense.

The math, in plain words

Three numbers decide what a missed call is worth to you:

  1. How many calls you miss. Calls a week times the share that go unanswered.
  2. How often a call becomes a job. If four in ten answered calls turn into work, assume the same for the ones you missed.
  3. What a job is worth. Your average invoice.

Missed calls times the close rate gives jobs at risk. Jobs at risk times the average job gives revenue at risk.

A worked example

InputExample
Calls a week30
Share missed40%, or 12 calls a week
Share of calls that become work40%
Average job value$450
Jobs at risk a monthabout 21
Revenue at risk a yearabout $112,000

That figure is a ceiling, not a forecast. Some missed callers do try again. The research below suggests fewer do than most owners expect.

What the research says

  • 62% of calls to small businesses went unanswered in a 30-day study of 85 businesses across 58 industries. Only 37.8% were picked up by a person. 411 Locals, 2016
  • More than half of homeowners expect a reply within the hour, and 28% expect one right away. Only about 1 in 5 home service businesses respond that fast. Jobber Home Service Trends Report, 2026
  • Reaching a lead within an hour makes you nearly 7 times more likely to qualify it than waiting even an hour longer. Harvard Business Review, 2011

What to do about it

  • Make sure every caller hears back fast. A quick text, a call back, or someone else picking up. What matters is that they hear from you before they call someone else.
  • Keep every call in one place. Names, numbers and messages saved where you'll see them, so nothing depends on a sticky note.
  • Decide who answers when you can't. A simple rule for who picks up during jobs, after hours and on weekends.

We set all three up as part of the phone piece of an operations review.

What the research found

FindingSource
Firms that tried to contact a lead within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited even an hour longer, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited a day. Across 2,241 companies, the average response time was 42 hours, and 23% never responded.Harvard Business Review, 2011
The odds of reaching a lead drop 100 times between a 5-minute response and a 30-minute one. The odds of qualifying it drop 21 times.InsideSales.com and MIT, Lead Response Management Study, 2007
More than half of homeowners expect a reply within the hour, and 28% expect one right away. Only about 20% of home service businesses respond within the hour.Jobber Home Service Trends Report, 2026

Why speed matters so much

A person who just filled out a form or left a message is sitting with the problem in front of them, phone in hand. An hour later they're on to the next thing, or on to the next business. The first company to reply usually gets the conversation, and the conversation is where the job gets decided.

How to hit it without living on your phone

  • Automatic text-back on missed calls. The caller hears from you in under a minute even when you're on a ladder. That buys you the time to call back properly.
  • An instant reply to web forms. "Got your message, we'll call you before 5 today." Then make sure someone does.
  • Quote templates on your phone. Most quotes can be sent from the driveway if the pricing and wording are already set up.
  • One inbox for calls, texts and forms. If leads land in five places, some of them wait. If they land in one, the oldest one is always at the top.
  • A rule for who answers. During jobs, after hours, on weekends. Written down, so it isn't a daily decision.

This is the first thing we look at in an operations review, because it's usually the fastest win in the business.

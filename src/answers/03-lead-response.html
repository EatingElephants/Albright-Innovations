What the research found

FindingSource
Firms that reached a new lead within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to qualify it as firms that reached out an hour later. They were more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited a day. Across 2,241 companies, the average response time was 42 hours, and 23% never responded.Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, Harvard Business Review, March 2011
The odds of reaching a lead drop 100 times between a 5-minute response and a 30-minute one. The odds of qualifying it drop 21 times.InsideSales.com and MIT, Lead Response Management Study, 2007
About 20% of home service businesses respond to leads within an hour. Survey of 1,050 home service businesses, December 2025.Jobber Home Service Trends Report, 2026

Why speed matters so much

The research above shows the odds of reaching and qualifying a lead fall quickly as the minutes pass. In the InsideSales.com and MIT study, the odds of reaching a lead dropped 100 times between a five-minute and a thirty-minute response.

How to hit it without living on your phone

  • A quick reply to missed calls. Even a short message tells the caller you'll be in touch, and buys you the time to call back properly.
  • An instant reply to web forms. "Got your message, we'll call you before 5 today." Then make sure someone does.
  • Fewer steps between the visit and the quote. When your pricing and wording are already set up, a quote doesn't have to wait for a free evening.
  • One place to check for calls, texts and forms. If leads land in five places, some of them wait. If they land in one, the oldest one is always at the top.
  • A rule for who answers. During jobs, after hours, on weekends. Written down, so it isn't a daily decision.

This is the first thing we look at in an operations check.

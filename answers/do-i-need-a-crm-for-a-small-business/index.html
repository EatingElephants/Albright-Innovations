Do I need a CRM for a small business, and what's the simplest place to start?

If you have more than a few dozen customers, yes, but it doesn't need to be fancy. A CRM is a customer list with the history attached, and the simplest place to start is one shared spreadsheet with six columns.

Last updated October 4, 2026

What a CRM is, in plain words

CRM stands for customer relationship management, which is a long name for a short idea: one list of everyone you've worked with or quoted, with what happened attached. Who they are, how to reach them, what they asked for, what you quoted, what they paid, and when you last talked.

If that information is currently split between your phone contacts, a notebook, your invoicing app and your memory, you don't have a CRM. You have four partial ones.

The simplest place to start

A shared spreadsheet with six columns. Nothing to buy, nothing to learn.

ColumnWhat goes in it
NameThe person, and the business if there is one
Phone and emailBoth, if you have both
AddressWhere the work happens
How they found youReferral, Facebook, ad, drive-by. Ask every time.
Last job and dateWhat you did and when
Next stepQuote to send, follow-up due, nothing for now

Fill it in for every call and every job for a month. That alone will change how you follow up.

When the spreadsheet stops being enough

  • You're typing the same customer into the spreadsheet, the calendar, the quote and the invoice
  • Follow-ups depend on someone remembering to look at the "next step" column
  • More than one person needs it at the same time, from a phone
  • You want to text or email past customers without copying numbers out by hand

At that point the right move is a simple customer-list app that connects to your phones, quoting and invoicing, so a name goes in once and shows up everywhere. There are plenty of good ones. The right one depends on the kind of work you do, and picking it is part of what we do in an operations review.

What we'd do

Pick the tool that fits how you already work, move your existing contacts into it, connect it to your phone and invoicing so it fills itself in, and show your team how to use it in an afternoon. The goal is a list nobody has to maintain by hand.

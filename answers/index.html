Answers

Questions owners ask us, answered straight.

Short answers here. Each one opens to a full page with the sources.

  • How much time do small business owners spend on admin?

    In one 2023 survey of 251 U.S. business owners, admin took 36% of a 45.5-hour work week, about 16 hours.

    Read the full answer →

  • What does a missed call cost a small business?

    A missed call costs you the chance at a job. To put a number on it, multiply the calls you miss by how often a call becomes work, then by your average job value. For a business getting 30 calls a week and missing 40% of them, that's roughly 21 jobs a month at risk.

    Read the full answer →

  • How fast should I respond to a new lead?

    Within five minutes if you can, and within the hour at the latest. In a study of 2,241 U.S. companies, those that reached a new lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as those that waited longer (Harvard Business Review, 2011).

    Read the full answer →

  • Do I need a CRM for a small business, and what's the simplest place to start?

    Our take: once you have more than a few dozen customers, it's worth having one, and it doesn't need to be fancy. A CRM is a customer list with the history attached, and the simplest place to start is one shared spreadsheet with six columns.

    Read the full answer →

  • How often should a local business post on Facebook?

    Our rule of thumb is at least once a week, and two to three times a week if you can keep it up. There's no study we know of that sets an exact number for local businesses. Hootsuite recommends brands post 1 to 2 times a day on Facebook, and says quality matters more than quantity (Hootsuite, 2025).

    Read the full answer →

  • Is it worth paying someone to manage our social media?

    It's worth it when the hours you'd spend are worth more than the fee, or when the posting isn't happening at all. Three hours a week at $75 an hour is $11,700 a year of your time.

    Read the full answer →

  • How do I know which ads or posts are bringing in customers?

    Give each ad or post its own trail: a separate phone number, a tagged link, or a "how did you hear about us" that gets written down every single time. Then match each job back to the trail it came in on.

    Read the full answer →

  • When is it time to redo my business website?

    When it no longer does its three jobs: tell people what you do, show them you're real and current, and make it easy to reach you. Our rule of thumb: if it's more than three years old, awkward on a phone, or shows old hours, prices or photos, it's worth a hard look.

    Read the full answer →

  • What should a small business website have on it?

    Six things: what you do, where you do it, proof you're real (your photos, your name, your reviews), a way to contact you on every page, current hours and prices where they apply, and a site that works on a phone.

    Read the full answer →

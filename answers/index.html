Answers

Questions owners ask us, answered straight.

Short answers here. Each one opens to a full page with the sources.

  • How much time do small business owners spend on admin?

    Most owners spend 15 to 20 hours a week on admin. One survey of 251 U.S. business owners put it at 36% of a 45.5-hour week, which works out to about 16 hours.

    Read the full answer →

  • What does a missed call cost a small business?

    A missed call costs you the chance of a job: the share of callers who don't try again, times how often a call becomes work, times your average job value. For a business getting 30 calls a week and missing 40% of them, that's roughly 21 jobs a month at risk.

    Read the full answer →

  • How fast should I respond to a new lead?

    Within five minutes if you can, and within the hour at the latest. Businesses that reach a new lead within an hour are nearly seven times more likely to qualify it than those that wait even an hour longer.

    Read the full answer →

  • Do I need a CRM for a small business, and what's the simplest place to start?

    If you have more than a few dozen customers, yes, but it doesn't need to be fancy. A CRM is a customer list with the history attached, and the simplest place to start is one shared spreadsheet with six columns.

    Read the full answer →

  • How often should a local business post on Facebook?

    At least once a week, so the page looks alive to someone checking you out, and two to three times a week if you want it to bring in work. Consistency matters more than volume.

    Read the full answer →

  • Is it worth paying someone to manage our social media?

    It's worth it when the hours you'd spend are worth more than the fee, or when the posting isn't happening at all. Three hours a week at $75 an hour is $11,700 a year of your time.

    Read the full answer →

  • How do I know which ads or posts are bringing in customers?

    Give each ad or post its own trail: a separate phone number, a tagged link, or a "how did you hear about us" that gets written down every single time. Then match each job back to the trail it came in on.

    Read the full answer →

  • When is it time to redo my business website?

    When it no longer does its three jobs: tell people what you do, show them you're real and current, and make it easy to reach you. If it's more than three years old, awkward on a phone, or shows old hours, prices or photos, it's time.

    Read the full answer →

  • What should a small business website have on it?

    Six things: what you do, where you do it, proof you're real (your photos, your name, your reviews), a way to contact you on every page, current hours and prices where they apply, and a site that works on a phone.

    Read the full answer →

    A walk-through of how work moves through your business, from the first call to the final invoice. The first conversation takes about 30 minutes, and you get a one-page list of what we'd fix first.
